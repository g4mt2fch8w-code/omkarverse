             CAPTIVE REPRODUCTION CHRONICLES & ROYAL ARCHIVAL BIOGRAPHY (1951–2026)

PREPARED FOR: Tiger Monitoring Repository & Longitudinal Carnivore Research Archive
HISTORICAL LOCATION: Bargadi Forests (Sidhi District) & Govindgarh Palace (Rewa District), Madhya Pradesh
CENTRAL FIGURE: Mohan (Wild-Born White Bengal Tiger, c. September 1950 – December 18, 1969)
PATRON & ARCHIVIST: Maharaja Martand Singh Judeo (Last Ruling Maharaja of Rewa State, Baghelkhand)
HERITAGE STATUS: The Progenitor and Founding Sire of All Captive White Tigers in the Modern World


====================================================================================================
CHRONOLOGICAL MILESTONES: THE SAGA OF MOHAN (1950–2026)
====================================================================================================
  * September 1950: Estimated birth of a wild white male cub to a normal-coloured orange tigress in the 
    dense Sal forests of Bargadi, Sidhi district (Vindhyan hills / Sanjay-Dubri landscape).
  * May 27, 1951: The Bargadi Expedition. Maharaja Martand Singh Judeo of Rewa encounters the tigress 
    and four cubs. The mother and three normal cubs are shot; the ~9-month-old white male cub is spared, 
    captured alive using tracking nets, and transported to Govindgarh Palace.
  * 1951–1952: Mohan is housed in the expansive 150-foot open courtyard enclosure of Govindgarh Fort Palace. 
    He is christened 'Mohan' ('The Enchanter' / 'The Attractive One') and treated with royal care.
  * 1953–1956: The Begum Matings. Mohan is paired with 'Begum', a wild-caught normal orange tigress. 
    Across three litters (1953, 1955, 1956), Begum delivers 10 cubs—ALL NORMAL ORANGE in coat color, 
    demonstrating that the white trait is not dominant.
  * 1957: Begum passes away. Maharaja Martand Singh realizes that the offspring must carry a recessive gene.
  * October 30, 1958: The Miraculous Second Generation. Mohan is backcrossed with his own daughter, 
    'Radha' (an obligate carrier). Radha gives birth to FOUR WHITE CUBS: Raja (M), Rani (F), Sukeshi (F), 
    and Mohini (F)—the first captive-born white tigers in world history!
  * 1960: Global Dispersal Begins. White cub 'Mohini' is acquired by American businessman John Kluge 
    and gifted to the Smithsonian National Zoological Park in Washington, D.C., presented directly to 
    President Dwight D. Eisenhower.
  * 1960–1964: Subsequent litters born to Mohan and Radha yield both white and orange cubs (including 
    Chameli, who is transferred to Bristol Zoo, UK in 1963).
  * December 18, 1969: Mohan passes away at Govindgarh Palace at the venerable age of ~19 years and 3 months. 
    He is accorded a 19-gun royal state salute and cremated/buried with full state honors.
  * April 3, 2016: The Return of the White Tiger. The Government of Madhya Pradesh inaugurates the 
    'Maharaja Martand Singh Judeo White Tiger Safari and Zoo' at Mukundpur (near Govindgarh/Rewa).
  * February 2025: The Central Zoo Authority (CZA) approves India's first dedicated White Tiger 
    Conservation Breeding Centre at Govindgarh, completing the historical circle.

SECTION 1: THE PRINCELY STATE OF REWA & THE BARGADI EXPEDITION
====================================================================================================

1.1 Baghelkhand & the Royal Shikar Tradition:
The Princely State of Rewa, ruled by the Baghel Rajput dynasty, comprised a vast expanse of rugged 
sandstone ridges, sal-covered plateaus, and perennial river valleys spanning modern-day Rewa, Satna, 
Sidhi, Singrauli, and Shahdol districts. For centuries, Rewa's monarchs were passionate naturalists and 
hunters who maintained extensive royal game preserves ('Shikargahs').
The sal-dominated forests of the Kaimur Range and the Son River basin harboured one of the densest tiger 
populations on the Indian subcontinent. Crucially, the Baghelkhand forest tract possessed a unique genetic 
reservoir of the recessive white tiger allele, with documented sightings and hunting trophies dating back 
over two centuries before Mohan's capture.

1.2 The Fateful Day: May 27, 1951:
In late May 1951, royal trackers reported the presence of a resident tigress accompanied by four large cubs 
in the Bargadi forest beating area (Compartment 42, Sidhi Forest Division, approximately 20 km southeast 
of Govindgarh, within the landscape continuum that today forms Sanjay-Dubri Tiger Reserve).
Maharaja Martand Singh Judeo organized a royal shikar party. Upon cornering the family group in a rocky 
sal nallah, the hunting party observed an astonishing sight: three of the cubs were normally striped orange, 
but the fourth was chalk-white with black stripes and brilliant pale eyes.

In accordance with the tragic hunting mores of the era, the mother tigress and the three normal orange cubs 
were shot. However, Maharaja Martand Singh strictly forbade his gun-bearers from firing upon the white cub. 
The ~9-month-old cub fled into a deep sandstone cleft. The Maharaja summoned local Baiga and Kol tribal trackers, 
who constructed heavy hemp rope nets and wooden drop-traps. After a tense 24-hour siege, the young white tiger 
was safely secured in a bamboo and timber crate without sustaining serious injury.

SECTION 2: LIFE AT GOVINDGARH FORT PALACE & ROYAL CAPTIVITY
====================================================================================================

2.1 The Govindgarh Courtyard Enclosure:
Mohan was transported to Govindgarh Fort, the Maharaja's palatial summer residence situated on the shores 
of the picturesque Govindgarh Lake. A vast, open-air stone courtyard adjacent to the royal apartments was 
converted into a secure, naturalistic enclosure:
  * Dimensions: Approximately 150 feet by 80 feet, enclosed by 20-foot dressed sandstone walls topped 
    with heavy iron mesh.
  * Internal Habitat: Provided with shade-giving neem and banyan trees, a freshwater masonry bathing pool 
    replenished daily from Govindgarh lake, and a sheltered sleeping den within the palace pavilion.
  * Royal Care: Mohan was provided with an attendant staff of eight keepers headed by master shikari 
    Babu Lal. He was nourished on a diet of 12 to 14 kilograms of fresh dressed mutton, goat meat, and beef 
    daily, supplemented with cod-liver oil, bone meal, and fresh mineral water.

2.2 Temperament and Behavioral Demeanor:
Within eighteen months of capture, Mohan grew into a magnificent prime adult male weighing over 220 kg 
(485 lbs) and measuring 9 feet 7 inches from nose to tail tip. 
Contrary to early fears that his conspicuous coloration was accompanied by neurological defects, Mohan 
was remarkably intelligent, physically robust, agile, and possessed an imposing, calm disposition. 
He developed a profound bond of recognition with Maharaja Martand Singh, frequently greeting the Maharaja's 
approach with deep territorial chuffing (prusten) and rubbing his massive head against the iron mesh.

SECTION 3: THE CAPTIVE BREEDING PROGRAMME & GENEALOGICAL RECONSTRUCTION
====================================================================================================

3.1 Phase I: Pairing with Begum (1952–1956) — The Mystery of Dominance:
Eager to establish a self-sustaining strain of white tigers, Maharaja Martand Singh acquired 'Begum', 
a wild-caught, normal orange tigress captured in the nearby forests of Sidhi:
  * First Mating (February 1953): Produced a litter of 3 cubs in May 1953 — 2 males, 1 female. ALL ORANGE.
  * Second Mating (1955): Produced a litter of 4 cubs — 2 males, 2 females. ALL ORANGE.
  * Third Mating (1956): Produced a litter of 4 cubs — 1 male, 3 females. ALL ORANGE.
  
Outcome: Over three successive litters, Begum delivered 11 cubs (1 died in infancy; 10 survived), not a single 
one of which was white. While many royal advisors believed the white coloration was an unrepeatable fluke of nature, 
the Maharaja and wildlife consultants consulted international geneticists, who deduced that the white coat was 
an autosomal recessive trait: Mohan was homozygous recessive (ww), Begum was homozygous dominant (WW), making 
all their offspring obligate heterozygous carriers (Ww) showing normal orange phenotypes.

3.2 Phase II: Backcrossing with Radha (1958) — The Historic Breakthrough:
Following Begum's death in 1957, Maharaja Martand Singh decided to test the recessive hypothesis by mating 
Mohan with his own daughter from the 1956 litter, a healthy, vigorous orange tigress named 'Radha'.
Radha was introduced to Mohan's enclosure in early 1958:
  * The October 30, 1958 Litter:
    After a 104-day gestation, Radha gave birth to four healthy cubs. When the attendants peered into the den, 
    they witnessed a zoological miracle: ALL FOUR CUBS WERE SNOW-WHITE WITH DARK STRIPES!
  * The Four Historic White Cubs:
      1. Raja (Male): Robust, broad-skulled white male; retained at Govindgarh and later Delhi Zoo.
      2. Rani (Female): Elegant white female; retained for breeding at Govindgarh.
      3. Sukeshi (Female): Vigorous white female; later paired with Mohan in 1963.
      4. Mohini (Female): 'The Enchantress'; possessed exceptional symmetry and beauty; selected for international transfer.

3.3 Subsequent Litters of Mohan and Radha:
  * May 1960 Litter: 4 cubs born — 2 white (Chameli [F] and Niladri [M]) and 2 normal orange.
  * March 1962 Litter: 4 cubs born — 2 white (Himalaya [M] and Ramesh [M]) and 2 normal orange (Gita [F], Sita [F]).
  * 1964 Litter: 4 cubs born — 2 white (Anarkali [F], Vikram [M]) and 2 normal orange.

3.4 Total Lifetime Reproductive Output of Mohan:
Over his 18 years in captivity, Mohan fathered a grand total of 34 cubs across 7 documented litters:
  * 21 White Cubs
  * 13 Normal Orange Cubs (all genetic carriers)
Every white tiger alive in the world today carries the direct autosomal DNA of Mohan and Radha.

SECTION 4: GLOBAL DISPERSAL & THE INTERNATIONAL EMBASSY OF WHITE TIGERS
====================================================================================================

4.1 Mohini's Voyage to the Smithsonian National Zoo (1960):
In 1960, through the mediation of prominent conservationist Ralph Graham and the American businessman 
John W. Kluge (Metromedia founder), the white female cub 'Mohini' was purchased from Maharaja Martand Singh 
for $10,000 and donated to the Smithsonian National Zoological Park in Washington, D.C.
  * White House Reception: On December 5, 1960, Mohini was officially presented to President Dwight D. Eisenhower 
    on the South Lawn of the White House before being transferred to the National Zoo.
  * American Dynasty: In Washington, Mohini was paired with 'Samson', a wild-caught normal orange Bengal 
    carrier male. She gave birth to numerous litters, including famous offspring 'Ramana' (M), 'Kesari' (F), 
    and 'Rewati' (F). Her lineage laid the genetic foundation for all white tigers across North American zoos, 
    including Cincinnati Zoo, San Diego Zoo, and the private lines later made famous in Las Vegas.

4.2 Chameli to Bristol Zoo, United Kingdom (1963):
In 1963, Mohan's daughter 'Chameli' was purchased by the Bristol, Clifton and West of England Zoological 
Society for £5,000. Her arrival in the UK created an international sensation, establishing the foundational 
white tiger breeding line in Europe.

4.3 Delhi Zoo (National Zoological Park) & Indian Breeding Hub:
In India, the National Zoological Park (Delhi Zoo) became the primary domestic repository for Mohan's lineage. 
Tigers Raja, Rani, Virat, Roop, Champak, Bhim, and Shweta founded prolific breeding bloodlines that supplied 
zoological parks in Kolkata (Alipore), Mumbai (Byculla), Hyderabad (Nehru Zoo), and Mysore (Sri Chamarajendra).


====================================================================================================
SECTION 5: THE PASSING OF MOHAN & STATE MEMORIALS
====================================================================================================

5.1 The Death of Mohan (December 18, 1969):
By late 1969, Mohan had reached the advanced age of 19 years and 3 months (an extraordinary lifespan for 
a large pantherine carnivore). In his final weeks, he suffered from progressive renal failure, severe osteoarthritis, 
and tooth wear.
On December 18, 1969, surrounded by his lifelong caretakers and Maharaja Martand Singh, Mohan slipped away peacefully.

5.2 Royal Funeral & Memorial Cenotaph:
Upon the announcement of Mohan's death:
  * Rewa City and Govindgarh observed an official day of state mourning; markets and government offices closed.
  * Thousands of local citizens and forest villagers gathered at Govindgarh Palace to pay homage.
  * The Maharaja ordered a 19-gun salute to be fired in his honour.
  * Mohan was wrapped in royal silk brocade, anointed with sandalwood paste, and cremated/buried with full 
    Vedic rites in the palace orchard.
  * A white marble 'Chhatri' (royal memorial pavilion) was constructed over his burial site in Govindgarh Fort.
  * His skin and skeletal mount were painstakingly taxidermied by Van Ingen & Van Ingen of Mysore and now 
    stand as the central exhibit in the Museum of the City Palace of Rewa.
