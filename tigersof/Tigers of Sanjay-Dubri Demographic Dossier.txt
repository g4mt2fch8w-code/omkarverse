====================================================================================================
DEMOGRAPHIC TIMELINE: THE POPULATION RECOVERY TRAJECTORY (2000–2026)
====================================================================================================
  * 2000: Post-Bifurcation Nadir. State reorganisation split undivided Sanjay National Park (1,938 km²); 
    the MP portion was left with 466.88 km² in Sanjay NP and 345.70 km² in Dubri WLS. Heavy biotic 
    pressures, cattle grazing, and poaching reduced tigers to sporadic, unmonitored individuals.
  * 2008: Statutory Tiger Reserve Notification (812.58 km² core). Systematic anti-poaching wireless 
    networks, foot patrolling beats, and protection chowkis established.
  * 2014 (AITE 3rd Cycle): Only 6 photographic detections from 27 camera pairs; breeding status uncertain; 
    classified as an under-stocked sink habitat reliant on occasional dispersers from Bandhavgarh.
  * 2018 (AITE 4th Cycle): 375 camera trap stations, 13,853 trap nights. 5 adult tigers identified 
    (SECR Density: 0.23 ± 0.10 / 100 km²; female-biased sex ratio). High abundance of Nilgai (10.08 / km²) 
    and Chital (9.67 / km²).
  * 2021: The 'Mausi Maa' (T-28) Milestone. Tigress T-28 adopted 4 orphaned cubs of her deceased sister, 
    successfully rearing 7 cubs simultaneously in Dubri core—igniting national media attention and 
    accelerating recruitment.
  * 2022 (AITE 5th Cycle): 573 camera trap stations, 17,011 trap nights. 874 tiger photo-detections; 
    16 unique adult tigers (>1 yr) identified. SECR Density surged to 0.78 ± 0.20 / 100 km² (over 3-fold increase).
  * 2022–2024: Prey Augmentation Revolution. Reintroduction of wild Gaur from Kanha and Satpura; translocation 
    of Chital from Bandhavgarh using the Boma method; voluntary village relocations creating large inviolate meadows.
  * 2024–2026: Consolidation & Transboundary Dispersal. Estimated 20–25+ resident adult tigers. High breeding 
    activity across Dubri, Bastua, and Badkadha, with resident subadults colonizing Guru Ghasidas NP in Chhattisgarh.

PART 1: THE LEGENDARY MATRIARCH — T-28 ('MAUSI MAA') & THE 7-CUB ADOPTION SAGA
====================================================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. T-28 / 'MAUSI MAA' (The Iconic Matriarch of Dubri Core)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Code: T-28 / SNJY_F28 / DUBRI_F01
  * Sex: Female (F)
  * Estimated Birth: Circa 2015–2016 (Wild-born in Dubri core)
  * Dominant Territory: Central Dubri Wildlife Sanctuary (Dubri Lake, Chamraval meadow, Bastua beat junction, 
    and tourist safari circuit)
  * Identifying Field Marks: Bold, symmetrical 'wishbone' mark above the right eye, clean white underbelly 
    flank, highly relaxed demeanor around departmental patrol vehicles and tourist gypsies.

  * The Historic 2021 Adoption Saga:
    In late 2020, T-28 gave birth to a healthy biological litter of three cubs (2 females, 1 male; sired by T-26). 
    Simultaneously, her biological sister—another resident breeding tigress occupying an overlapping home range 
    along the Banas river edge—gave birth to a litter of four cubs.
    Tragedy struck when the sister mysteriously went missing (presumed dead due to natural conflict or snakebite), 
    leaving her four ~2-month-old cubs stranded, emaciated, and vulnerable to infanticide.
    In an event unprecedented in recorded carnivore biology, T-28 encountered the orphaned cubs and, instead of 
    displaying territorial aggression or infanticide, gently accepted them into her den. She allowed all four 
    orphans to suckle alongside her own three biological cubs, grooming, protecting, and leading the massive 
    super-litter of 7 cubs across the Dubri sal forests.
    
    The local forest staff and naturalists christened her 'Mausi Maa' (Hindi for Maternal Aunt / Mother). 
    For over 18 months, T-28 faced immense metabolic demands, executing near-daily kills of adult nilgai, wild boar, 
    and chital to satiate the voracious appetites of seven rapidly growing cubs. Against all statistical odds, 
    under intensive 24/7 protection by dedicated MP Forest Department tracking teams, ALL SEVEN CUBS SURVIVED 
    to independent sub-adulthood!
    
  * Reproductive & Demographic Legacy:
      - 2021 Super-Litter (7 Cubs Raised to Independence):
          * T-28_C1 (Male, biological son; dispersed to western Dubri buffer).
          * T-28_C2 (Female, biological daughter; resident in Chamraval).
          * T-28_C3 (Female, biological daughter; now T-30, resident breeding female).
          * T-28_AC1 (Male, adopted nephew; dispersed into Shahdol forest division).
          * T-28_AC2 (Female, adopted niece; now T-32, resident in Bastua range).
          * T-28_AC3 (Male, adopted nephew; transboundary dispersal into Guru Ghasidas NP).
          * T-28_AC4 (Female, adopted niece; resident along Gopad river).
      - 2024 Litter (2 Cubs):
          * T-28_24C1 (Male cub, ~1.5 years old, flourishing).
          * T-28_24C2 (Female cub, ~1.5 years old, flourishing).
  * Ecological Impact: Mausi Maa's miraculous foster-rearing single-handedly transformed the demographic 
    vitality of Sanjay-Dubri, elevating the park into an internationally recognized bastion of carnivore conservation.

PART 5: HISTORICAL FOUNDATION & THE WHITE TIGER PHENOMENON
====================================================================================================

5.1 The Historic May 1951 Event in Bargadi Forest:
On May 27, 1951, a hunting expedition led by Maharaja Martand Singh of Rewa in the Bargadi forest tract 
(near Govindgarh, Sidhi district, within the present-day Sanjay-Dubri landscape) resulted in the shooting 
of a normal-coloured tigress accompanied by four cubs. Three cubs were normal orange, but one male cub was 
pure white with blackish-brown stripes and icy blue eyes.
The Maharaja spared and captured the 9-month-old cub, naming him 'Mohan'. Mohan was brought to the Govindgarh 
Palace, where a dedicated courtyard enclosure was constructed for his maintenance.

5.2 Genetics of the Rewa White Tigers:
Mohan was initially mated with a normal wild-caught orange tigress named 'Begum', producing normal orange 
offspring (including 'Radha'). In 1958, Mohan was backcrossed with his orange daughter Radha. On October 30, 1958, 
Radha gave birth to four cubs—all four were white! This confirmed that the white phenotype is inherited as an 
autosomal recessive trait.
Modern molecular genomics has confirmed that the white coat mutation is caused by an amino acid substitution 
in the SLC45A2 transporter gene, which selectively blocks the synthesis of yellow/red pheomelanin without 
disrupting dark eumelanin stripe synthesis. The moist and dry sal ecosystems of Sanjay-Dubri provided the original 
evolutionary crucible for this recessive allele in the wild population.
